# Macro Economy & Factions Module Design

## Overview
The `macro-sim` module is the backbone of the "living universe". It tracks commodity supply/demand across star systems and simulates the physical movement of thousands of NPC ships outside of the player's localized Box2D physics bubble. Movement follows `documentation/orbital-mechanics/Design.md`: every body outside the bubble is on a Keplerian orbit at 1:1 real-world scale.

## Implementation Dependencies
- **`core-engine`**: Uses the Ashley ECS, but heavily leverages background threads.
- **`orbital` math package** (shared with `physics-exterior`): `stateAt(t)`, `elementsFrom(r, v)`, Kepler solver, SOI prediction.
- **`physics-exterior`**: **Hard Dependency for Transitions.** The system needs the bubble's orbital anchor state (kept by `PhysicsManager`) to handle the injection of macro-ships into the active physics bubble when they get too close.

## Implementation Guide
To implement this module independently (mocking the Box2D transition logic):

1. **On-Rails Movement and Background Prediction:**
   - Every macro entity carries an `OrbitComponent` (2D Keplerian elements around a parent body, with an epoch). Its position at any sim time is `stateAt(t)`, computed analytically in doubles. Nothing is stepped per tick, so there is no drift and time warp costs nothing extra.
   - Changes of course are events in a sim-time priority queue: maneuver nodes (impulsive burns), SOI transitions and predicted surface impacts. Elements change only when an event fires, on the main thread.
   - A dedicated `ExecutorService` handles the expensive *predictions*, not stepping: finding which bodies may enter the player bubble soon, the warp drop-out point for the next encounter, and positions for map/HUD rendering. It reads immutable snapshots of the elements and hands results back to the main thread at a tick boundary.
   - NPC AI plans travel as maneuver nodes (e.g. a transfer burn and a circularisation burn). When the NPC is in the player bubble, the same plan is flown by a controller with real thrust in Box2D.

2. **Economy Simulation:**
   - Create `EconomyNode` POJOs attached to stations. These contain maps of `CommodityType` to `InventoryQuantity`.
   - On a slow timer (e.g., every 5 minutes), run a pass to consume resources (e.g., population consumes food/water) and produce goods based on station type. Update base prices based on supply/demand curves.
   - Generate `TradeOrder` objects. Idle macro-freighters will pick up orders, set their destination, and travel.

3. **Bubble Transitions (Main Thread Sync):**
   - Create a `BubbleTransitionSystem` in the main ECS loop. Distances are measured to the bubble's orbital **anchor**, not to the player.
   - Candidates come from the background prediction (bodies that could reach the bubble before the next check), so the main thread checks only a short list.
   - **Entry:** within 20 km of the anchor and relative speed below about 1,000 m/s: evaluate `stateAt(t)`, subtract the anchor state in doubles, and create the Box2D body at that relative position and velocity. The entity and its UUID persist; only the physics representation changes. Its pending rails events are cancelled (maneuver nodes become AI goals).
   - **Fast pass:** within 20 km but faster than about 1,000 m/s: the body becomes a kinematic transient (drawn and swept for collisions by `physics-exterior`) and stays on rails until it slows down or leaves.
   - **Exit:** beyond 25 km from the anchor: convert the Box2D state back to absolute doubles, compute new orbital elements, destroy the body, and enqueue the body's next rails events. The 5 km gap between entry and exit stops bodies flipping at the boundary.

## Implemented Components
- `EconomyNodeComponent`: marks a station entity as part of the economy.
- `FreighterComponent`: an NPC hauler that takes trade orders, by UUID.
- `TraderComponent`: credits and cargo of a ship that trades by hand (the player's).
- `HostileComponent` (`hostile` package): a pirate or patrol on the rails: role, state, faction, home station or patrol route, target, loot and firepower. Saved with its entity.
- The rails components, `OrbitComponent` (Keplerian elements, parent UUID, epoch) and `ManeuverNodeComponent` (scheduled burns), belong to orbital-mechanics.

## Implemented Systems
- `MacroSimSystem`: the `FixedLogicSystem` that advances `MacroSimulation` (economy, freighters, rails events and bubble transitions) to the sim clock.
- `EncounterPredictionTask`: Background predictions for bubble entry, warp drop-out and map rendering.
- `BubbleTransitionSystem`: Moves ships between the background math simulation and the active Box2D simulation. Not an ECS system: `MacroSimulation` runs it at each sim second.
- `HostileTrafficController`: plans pirates and patrols on the rails, as `FreighterController` does freighters. Not an ECS system either; `MacroSimulation.hostiles()` runs it.
- `OrbitalEventSystem`, which fires due rails events, belongs to orbital-mechanics.

## Prototype Status
The first prototype lives in `macro-sim/src/main/java/com/ringsofiron/macrosim`. Run `gradlew :macro-sim:runMacroDemo` (optionally `-Pdays=60`) for a headless 30-day report, and `gradlew :macro-sim:test` for the checks.

- **Rails** are reached through the `RailsModel` interface. `OrbitalRailsModel` is the real one: elements in `OrbitComponent`, burns in `ManeuverNodeComponent`, fired with SOI changes and impacts by orbital-mechanics' `OrbitalEventSystem` on the model's own event-by-event clock. `StubRailsModel`/`StubKeplerOrbit` are a dependency-free one-level stand-in used by the headless demo and most tests.
- **Economy:** `EconomySimulation` runs the 5-minute pass (production cycles limited by inputs and storage, daily upkeep, price curve `base · (target / stock)^0.7` clamped to 0.25–4×) and rebuilds the `TradeOrder` board. Definitions come through `CommodityCatalog`: `DefRegistryCommodityCatalog` reads modding-data's `CommodityDef`/`StationTypeDef` (`assets/data/economy.json` plus mods, one def cycle = one production cycle, 4 per day), and `DefaultCommodityCatalog` is a small built-in set used by the demo and tests.
- **Freighters:** a freighter's hold is a volume: `holdCapacity()` gives it in m³ and `capacityUnits` turns it into units of one commodity by the commodity's volume per unit. The game sizes it from the bays of the freighter's chassis. `FreighterController` scores open orders by profit per hour, then flies each leg as maneuver nodes from `TransferPlanner` (phased Hohmann transfer, or a phasing orbit on the same radius) and loads or unloads on arrival. Legs to another body go through `RoutePlanner` (patched conics, one SOI step: out to a moon or down to its parent). Going out, a Hohmann transfer to the moon's orbit is started a few seconds early or late so the ship passes the moon at the station's radius, circling the same way; coming down, an escape burn is timed so the fall reaches the station's radius around the parent. Only the burn before the SOI crossing is planned ahead: once the crossing happens (checked by a macro event just after the predicted time), capture at periapsis and the transfer to the station are planned from the real orbit, so errors do not pile up. Order scoring uses a cheap estimate of each leg. Phasing orbits never dip within 200 km of the surface.
- **Player trading:** `MacroSimulation.dockableStation(shipUuid)` names the nearest station within 500 m and 10 m/s of a ship, in the bubble or on rails. `StationMarket` (`MacroSimulation.market()`) lists a station's board as `MarketQuote`s and buys or sells for a `TraderComponent` (credits and cargo, saved like any component). Cargo takes up volume: each `CommodityType` has m³ and kg per unit. A trader with a `CargoHold` attached (the game attaches the player's interior-grid cargo bays) buys only what fits in it and the goods go into it; without one it keeps an abstract hold of `cargoCapacity` m³. Trades walk the station's price curve and update its prices at once, a 5% spread on each side keeps round trips from paying, and stock promised to freighters is never sold. Stations also buy salvaged parts (`part:<component>` crates) at a twentieth of their price but never sell them (round 2, `SalvageSettlement` in core).
- **Losses:** `MacroSimulation.destroy(uuid)` takes a station or freighter out cleanly: its claimed orders are cancelled and what they held at the other station is released, a lost freighter's cargo goes with it, and freighters bound for a lost station fly to the nearest remaining one and sell or dump the cargo there. Surface impacts from the rails (`RailsModel.addImpactListener`, fed by `OrbitalEventSystem.impacts`) call it on their own, between events, and the entity is removed.
- **Bubble:** `EncounterPredictionTask` (background `ExecutorService`) culls by radius band and then uses conservative advancement, so it cannot step over an encounter; its earliest entry is the warp drop-out time. A background forecast is taken up at the next prediction slot (every `PREDICTION_INTERVAL`), waiting for the thread if it is still running, never as soon as it happens to finish, so thread timing cannot change who enters the bubble; the game runs prediction inline anyway. `BubbleTransitionSystem` does entry, fast pass and exit against the anchor through the `BubbleGateway` interface: `PhysicsBubbleGateway` is the real one over physics-exterior (`setTrueState`/`getTrueState`, `KinematicTransientComponent`, body building via a `BodyBuilder` that `RailsBubbleHandoff.rebuild` can fill), and `MockBubbleGateway` is a Box2D-free stand-in for the headless demo. Stations always stay on rails and show in the bubble as kinematic transients; with stations-docking they get a kinematic body shaped like their grid. After a load, fast passes are not restored; they start again from the next forecast.
- **Time:** `MacroSimSystem` is a `FixedLogicSystem` on core-engine's `SimClock`; each tick and each rails-warp `catchUp` calls `MacroSimulation.advanceTo(simSeconds)`, which fires rails and macro events in time order, so one rails-warp jump gives the same economy as many small steps. A burn or orbit change planned by an event inside one jump counts from that event's time, not the jump's end (round 7, `OrbitalEventSystem.orbitChanged(entity, t)`; `RailsModel.doneAdvancing` ends the jump), so it fires when due: before, burns planned during the tow's three-day skip all fired at its end. It runs after physics-exterior's step and floating-origin rebase, so handoffs read the anchor at the clock's sim second.
- **Pirates and patrols** (round 2, `claude/r2-roster-traffic-z8x44s`): `MacroSimulation.addPirate` and `addPatrol` put a `HostileComponent` ship on the rails, and `HostileTrafficController` plans it.
  - **Pirates:** a pirate holds an orbit 60 km off a station on a trade lane. Every 15 minutes it looks for prey.
    - It hunts the player only when bold enough (firepower at least `PLAYER_NERVE` 0.8 of the player's), when the player is on a near-circular orbit (`MAX_PLAYER_ECCENTRICITY` 0.02) of the same body, and once the game's grace period is over.
    - Otherwise it goes for the loaded freighter with the most cargo that it can reach 300 s before the freighter does. It skips stations guarded by a patrol of equal or greater firepower.
    - It lies in wait 5 km off the freighter's destination and robs it on arrival (`FreighterController.plunder`: the order is cancelled, what it reserved is released, the hold is emptied and the freighter repositions). A patrol turning up drives it off (`drivenOff`).
    - It hunts for at most 6 hours, then goes home and rests for 6 hours.
  - **Patrols:** a patrol flies its faction's stations in turn and holds 3 km off each for 2 hours.
  - **In the bubble:** a hostile is flown by the game (`HostileTraffic` in core). On leaving the bubble it circularizes and goes home or on to its next stop.
  - **Saves and losses:** a load reschedules every hostile's plan (`restoreHostiles`, through `MacroSaveParticipant`), and `destroy` and surface impacts remove hostiles like freighters. The game's side of hostile traffic (top-ups, names, the intercept alert, the player's grace, bubble timers) is saved by `HostileTraffic` itself, under `"hostile-traffic"`; `playerGraceUntil` goes with it.
  - **Listener events:** `intercepting`, `plundered` and `drivenOff`, which the game turns into notices and warp drop-outs.
  - **Wingmen** (round 3): a pirate with `wingOf` set lurks `WING_SPACING` (2 km) behind its leader and never hunts on its own. When its leader commits to prey it goes too, 2 km further back. The pair's firepower together is what the player's is weighed against, so two raiders dare what one would not. A wingman whose leader is lost flies alone.
  - **Moored** (round 6): a hostile with `mooring` set (a pirate base's gun platform) has no schedule and holds `mooring` meters behind its station on the station's orbit. It never hunts, robs or counts as traffic, and when it leaves the bubble it goes back to its mooring.
  - **Hold sides** (round 7): a hold closer than `CLEAR_SIDE` (20 km) is taken on the side the ship's transfer reaches without crossing the station. A ship coming up from a lower orbit holds ahead of the station and one coming down holds behind it (`arrivalSide`); before leaving for another orbit, a ship whose departure would cross the station first moves to the mirror hold (`departureSide`, `leaveHold`). Before, a pirate laying an ambush at Deepwell and later burning home flew straight through the depot at more than 1 km/s.
  - **Tows** (round 7): `playerTakenAway(now, until)` drops every hostile hunting the player (surrendered prizes aside), sends a pirate already on its way home, and keeps the player's grace until the tug has the ship in. Core's `Rescue.tow` calls it before the skip, and the grace and dropped targets are saved as before.
  - **Grudges** (round 3): an ace with `grudge` hunts the player whatever the odds (`PLAYER_NERVE` is not checked), still only after the grace period. `HostileComponent` also carries `loadout`, `ace`, `escapes` and `partDropped`, saved with its entity.
- **Contracts on the trade board** (round 2, `claude/r2-contracts-qf9mvv`): a delivery contract takes an open `TradeOrder` off the board for the player (`claimedBy = "player"`, and the buyer `expectIncoming`s the goods), so no freighter takes it. A contract that ends gives the order back: `close` for what was not delivered, and the buyer's expected stock is taken back. The contract rules themselves are in `documentation/progression/Design.md`.
- **In the game:** `GameWorld` builds the simulation on its own `RailsUniverse` and `OrbitalEventSystem`, with a gateway that reads the anchor's parent live from physics. Rails warp drops out at `nextDropOutTime`, which counts only bodies crossing into the bubble (bodies already inside, listed in `EncounterForecast.alreadyInside`, would otherwise stop every warp). While rails warp has the bubble collapsed, `setBubbleSuspended(true)` pauses entries and exits; resuming calls `BubbleTransitionSystem.reconcile`, which releases bodies the drop-out left on rails. Ships entering the bubble are flown by ai-crew's NPC pilot along their cancelled burns.
