# AI & Crew Simulation Module Design

## Overview
The `ai-crew` module is responsible for the autonomous behaviors of NPCs aboard ships and stations. It handles pathfinding across the 2D interior grids, priority-based task execution, and internal combat.

## Implementation Dependencies
- **`core-engine`**: Requires Ashley ECS.
- **`interior-grid`**: **Hard Dependency.** Pathfinding relies completely on querying the `ShipGridComponent` to determine walkable tiles, wall obstructions, and hazards.
- **External Libraries:** LibGDX `gdx-ai` extension (for A* pathfinding and Behavior Trees).

## Implementation Guide
To implement this module independently (mocking the `interior-grid` by feeding it a dummy 2D boolean array):

1. **A* Pathfinding Implementation:**
   - Implement the `IndexedGraph` interface from `gdx-ai`. The graph nodes are the grid tiles. A connection exists between two adjacent tiles when `ShipGridComponent.canPass(x, y, nx, ny)` is true and both tiles are open. Big machinery and stacked cargo block (`CrewObstacles` in interior-grid, the one place the rule lives): crew go round a reactor, shield emitter, turret mount or life support footprint, and round any cargo bay tile with goods on it, but walk over consoles (helm, weapons), airlocks, docking ports and empty bays. `GridNavGraph.isWalkable` applies it (the craft's `CargoHoldComponent` is looked up by `CrewService.pathfinder`), and `InteriorWorld` builds its walls from the same test, so an obstacle is a solid block crew bodies bump into and slide along. A crew member caught on a tile that becomes blocked (goods set down on it) walks off it without a body. `canPass` already handles full-tile walls, thin edge walls between tiles, open and sealed doors, and walls destroyed down to integrity 0 (which no longer block). Crew work a mounted system from a tile next to its footprint.
   - The grid follows Box2D's orientation (tile [0][0] bottom-left, +y up), so grid positions map to the ship body's local frame with no flip.
   - Rebuild affected connections when doors open or close and when walls are destroyed.
   - Create a `CrewMovementSystem` that walks crew along their path: each crew member is a body in a Box2D world inside the deck (see Interior physics below), steered toward the next tile at their movement speed.

2. **Job Blackboard System:**
   - Create a `ShipTaskBoardComponent` attached to the main ship entity. This contains priority queues of `Task` objects (e.g., `RepairTask(x, y)`, `ManConsoleTask(consoleId)`).
   - Tasks are generated by other systems (e.g., `GridHazardSystem` spots a fire and posts an `ExtinguishTask`).

3. **Behavior Tree Integration:**
   - Define a set of `Task` classes extending `gdx-ai`'s `LeafTask`.
   - **Evaluate & Claim:** An idle crew member evaluates the blackboard. Based on their assigned role (Engineer, Marine), they pop the highest priority relevant task.
   - **Execute:** The behavior tree transitions to executing the task (e.g., `Sequence( MoveTo(task.x, task.y), PlayAnimation(repairing), Wait(5s), CompleteTask() )`).

4. **Internal Combat Logic:**
   - Implement line-of-sight checks with a supercover grid traversal (Amanatides & Woo, the same `GridRayTraversal` combat uses), not Bresenham, so sight cannot slip between two diagonal walls. Intact full-tile walls and closed edge walls block sight; edge walls are checked whenever the ray crosses a tile boundary.
   - If a hostile entity is within LOS, the behavior tree interrupts the current task and pushes an `EngageCombatTask`.

5. **Crew Injury, Unconsciousness and Death:**
   Combat applies damage from penetrating hits, spall, fragments and interior blast (`combat-sys/Design.md` section 5.9). This module owns what that damage does:

   | State | Entered when | Effect |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Conscious** | Health above `unconsciousThreshold`, and no worse than a light wound | Normal (a light wound slows work and walking) |
   | **Unconscious** (down) | Health at or below `unconsciousThreshold`, or a serious or critical wound | Drops its current task and releases it to the board, cannot act or move on its own, still occupies its tile |
   | **Dead** | Health reaches 0, or a critical wound goes untreated too long | Removed from duty; the body stays on its tile |

   - **Wounds (round 5):** hits wound rather than take health. Rounds, spall, fragments, blasts and a laser's burn (`DamageCause.PENETRATOR`, `SPALL`, `FRAGMENT`, `BLAST`, `BURN`; `CrewService.wounds`) add their points to the crew member's `injury`, and the wound (`Wound`) is as bad as the injury: under 5 points from one hit is a graze and counts for nothing; light up to 70, serious from 70, critical from 90 (`CrewDef.seriousWound`, `criticalWound`). A hit of 200 or more (`lethalWound`: a warhead going off beside them, not a round) kills outright. So the 100 points a round does in the first room it crosses, which used to kill, now leaves a critical wound. Air, heat, fire and small arms still take health as before, so boarding fights go as they did.
     - **Light:** works and walks at `woundedWorkFactor` (0.7); heals by itself on a safe tile in `lightHealTime` (30 min).
     - **Serious:** down, waiting for help. Treatment brings it to light; with nobody to treat them it eases to light by itself on a safe tile in `seriousHealTime` (20 min), so a crew all down still comes round.
     - **Critical:** down and bleeding: dies after `bleedOutTime` (5 min) without treatment. The clock stops while someone treats them; a serious hit on someone already critical halves what is left.
   - **Stabilising (round 6):** a critical wound nobody has stabilised gets a `StabiliseTask` (kind `STABILISE`, priority 60, with putting out fires) wherever the casualty lies. Anyone takes it as keenly as a fire (at least 1; a medic 1.5): `CrewDef.stabiliseTime` (15 s) at baseline medicine, half that for a medic, scaled by the medicine skill factor and their work pace, four times as long with no medical stores left, and it uses one store. A stabilised wound (`CrewMemberComponent.stabilised`, saved) no longer bleeds out; it still needs treatment to come down a level, which clears it, and a new serious hit opens it again. A critical casualty on an unsafe tile is stabilised before a `RescueTask` is posted for them: with nowhere safe to carry them, the rescue used to leave them to bleed out.
   - **Treatment:** `CrewHealthSystem` posts a `TreatTask` for anyone down on a safe tile (a `RescueTask` first if the tile isn't safe). Medics want it most (1.5); engineers, marines and crewmen take it too (0.5). Each wound comes down a level for `CrewDef.careTime` seconds of a baseline medic's care (critical 60 s, serious 120 s, light 90 s), scaled by the carer's `CrewMemberComponent.careRate`: 1 for a medic at medicine skill 0.5, 0.4 for anyone else, times the medicine skill factor (0.6 to 1.4), morale and their own wound and suit. Each level uses one of the ship's medical stores (`ShipGridComponent.medicalStores`, 6 aboard; a yard restocks them); with none left care runs at a quarter speed (`NO_STORES_CARE`). A patient treated up from a serious wound to a light one gets up and goes back to work. The time spent treating is medicine experience (`CrewRecord.pendingCare`).
   - **Light wounds and the medbay (round 7):** a crew member on their feet with a light wound, on a safe tile and off battle stations, gets a `TreatTask` at `TreatTask.LIGHT_PRIORITY` (5). Anyone but the patient may take it, a medic keenest. It uses no stores (round 8's rate is under First aid, below), and the carer drops it for any other task (crew at a console never leave it for one). Alone and idle, the wounded treat themselves. A tile with the `MEDBAY` floor covering is a bed (`TaskRules.isMedbay`). Off battle stations, a casualty down on a safe tile gets a `MedbayCarryTask` (a `RescueTask` at priority 55) to a free bed, and anyone treating them in place hands over to it. Any care on a bed runs at `CrewDef.MEDBAY_CARE` (1.5x).
   - **First aid (round 8):** a light wound is first aid, which anyone gives at a medic's rate for their skill (`CrewMemberComponent.careRate(Wound)`). It runs at the full rate unless a worse wound aboard waits with nobody treating it, when it drops to `CrewDef.LIGHT_CARE` (half). Off battle stations, a lightly wounded crew member sat at a console sees to their own wound there, and a carer on a tile with good air takes their suit off while they treat (`suitTime` / 2).
   - **Prisoners** held aboard get rescue and treatment from the crew of the ship that holds them, like its own crew.
   - Unconscious crew still occupy their tile, so `GridOccupantQuery.at(x, y)` keeps returning them and further hits make the wound worse.
   - **Asphyxiation:** crew on a tile whose compartment oxygen ratio (`ShipGridComponent.oxygenRatioAt`) is below `asphyxiationThreshold` lose health each hazard tick, faster as oxygen falls. They pass out first and then die. A hull breach therefore threatens crew who were never in the line of fire.
   - **Rescue:** combat posts a `RescueTask` (carry an unconscious crew member to a pressurized tile) for unconscious crew in danger, and `CrewHealthSystem` posts one when crew pass out from asphyxiation. The rescuer moves at reduced speed while carrying, and drops the casualty if knocked out. With nowhere aboard fit to breathe (the whole ship holed), the rescuer puts the casualty's suit on them where they are (the rescuer's own `suitTime`) and tries again later (round 6). The player's figure is rescued like anyone else.
   - **Suits in a vented ship (round 9):** for the player's crew only (`TaskRules.SUIT_CARE_FACTION`; given to the aces' crews too, it cost the player four of twenty fights against the railgun ace), a hand at a console puts their suit on where they sit, still holding the post, while the ship is at battle stations with suits ordered or while their room is losing air below `TaskRules.SUIT_AT_POST_KPA` (50 kPa); a hand who took the console before battle stations were called used to sit on unsuited (`SuitUp` runs only as a task is taken) and suffocate as the cabin vented. With nowhere aboard to carry a casualty to, the rescuer puts their suit on them, and once that suit is below `suitRefillShare` carries them to an oxygen tank or working life support and fills it there (`suitRefillTime`), as crew fill their own. The progress of putting a suit on or taking it off at work (`CrewMemberComponent.suiting`, `unsuiting`) and the raked tiles (`ShipTaskBoardComponent.hitTiles`) are saved.
   - **The captain (round 10):** the player's figure is a crew entity with no tree. As a casualty it is carried and its suit filled like anyone's. Standing, `CaptainSafety` in core fills its suit beside the ship's oxygen (`CrewTasks.suitOxygenBeside`, at four minutes or less), and walks it aboard a docked craft with air. While it is walked from thin air, time warp drops to ×1. When it dies, crew of the ship's own sheltering where it is docked can bring it home (`Rescue`), as well as those aboard.
   - **Recovery (default until decided):** unconscious crew on a pressurized tile slowly regain health and wake once above `unconsciousThreshold` (and no worse than lightly wounded). A `TreatTask` speeds this up. Dead crew do not recover.
   - Thresholds and rates live in data (`CrewDef`), e.g. `unconsciousThreshold = 0.25`, `asphyxiationThreshold = 0.3`.

6. **Interfaces this module implements for `combat-sys`:**
   - `GridOccupantQuery.at(x, y)`: crew entities on a tile, conscious or not.
   - `CrewDamageSink.applyDamage(crewEntity, amount, cause)`: wounds (for hits) or subtracts health (for the rest) and moves the crew member between the states above.
   - `DamageTaskSink`: accepts `SealBreachTask`, `RepairTask`, `ExtinguishTask` and `RescueTask` onto the `ShipTaskBoardComponent`.
   - `ShipHazardScanSystem` also posts a `PatchLeakTask` (priority 70, an engineer's specialty) for each leak to space `LeakPatches` finds while the ship has patch kits. The crew member works it from beside the leaking wall, or reaches up to 4 tiles across machinery that stands against it (`TaskRules.REACH_ACROSS`; 4 since round 5, so a hull tile behind the corvette's reactor and nose turret can be patched from the bay beside them), takes `patchTime` (6 s, faster with engineering skill) and spends one kit.
   - It posts a `ResetShortTask` for every shorted system or conduit tile (priority 50, 85 for a reactor, since a shorted reactor blacks the ship out: round 8 put it above sealing breaches at 80, so a free engineer turns to a dark reactor first, and below rescues at 90; round 10: at battle stations the player's crews take a short on a system the ship fights with, `TaskRules.vital` (the reactor, shield emitters, gun mounts, helm, weapons console, engines and conduits), at 84 (`TaskRules.VITAL_RESET_PRIORITY`), also above sealing breaches, as seed 9's career lost Iron Dagny with its helm and gun mounts dark for 45 s while all four crew sealed breaches; and a `StabiliseTask` for one of the player's crews with `TaskRules.LAST_CHANCE` (120 s) or less to live is chosen at 110 (`TaskRules.URGENT_STABILISE_PRIORITY`), ahead of everything, as round 10's frigate raids had crew hit again where they lay bleed out with 34 to 66 s left; carrying casualties out of where the fire falls was tried as well and dropped, as it saved nobody on seeds 10 and 12 (the dead there were hit three to six times where they lay, the weight of the raid's fire); worked like a repair, or from on or beside a conduit; `resetTime` 5 s) and an `IsolateTankTask` for every leaking tank (priority 55; `isolateTime` 4 s), which shuts its valve so it stops leaking. Engineers want both most (1.5), technicians 1.2, crewmen 0.5. A repair to full health clears a short and opens an isolated tank again. See `documentation/interior-grid/Design.md`, "Damage states".

## Implemented Components
- `CrewMemberComponent`: name, role, faction, `CrewDef` stats, health, wound (with its injury points, bleed-out clock and treatment so far) and state (conscious / unconscious / dead), the claimed task, who they carry or are carried by, a load in hand, their home craft and the item in hand.
- `GridPositionComponent`: position in tile units on the ship they are aboard (`shipRef` saved), the path being walked, and the docked walkway they are out on.
- `BehaviorTreeComponent`: the crew member's `gdx-ai` behavior tree.
- `PlayerAvatarComponent`: marks the crew member moved by input instead of AI.
- `ShipTaskBoardComponent`: on the ship, the posted and claimed tasks.
- `NpcPilotComponent`, `NpcCombatComponent`: on a ship, its pilot's burns and its combat target.

## Implemented Systems
- `CrewMovementSystem`: Walks crew along the calculated A* path, and the avatar where its input points, as bodies in the deck's Box2D world.
- `CrewDecisionSystem`: Ticks the behavior trees for all conscious crew. Unconscious and dead crew are skipped.
- `CrewHealthSystem`: Applies asphyxiation and burns on the hazard tick, runs wounds (bleeding out, treatment, healing), handles recovery, and posts rescue and treatment tasks.
- `ShipHazardScanSystem`: Posts fire, breach and repair tasks from the grid and cancels stale ones. A breach is sealed from a tile beside it, or, like a leak, by reaching across a machine standing against it (up to `TaskRules.REACH_ACROSS` tiles): a hull holed behind an engine has no free tile beside it, and before round 6 nobody could seal it.
- `NpcPilotSystem`, `NpcCombatSystem`: Fly NPC ships through their burns, and fight a target.

## Prototype status
The first prototype is in `ai-crew/src/main/java/com/ringsofiron/aicrew`. `Crew demo.bat` (`gradlew :lwjgl3:runCrewDemo`) shows it running with mouse controls to start fires, breach the hull, wound crew and spawn boarders. Run `gradlew :ai-crew:runCrewConsoleDemo` for a headless run of a three-room ship (fire, damaged reactor, hull breach, two boarders) printed as text, and `gradlew :ai-crew:test` for the tests.

- **Shared interfaces** live in `interior-grid`, package `com.ringsofiron.interiorgrid.crew`, so `combat-sys` and the hazard simulation can use them without depending on `ai-crew`: `GridOccupantQuery.occupantsAt(ship, x, y, out)`, `CrewDamageSink.applyDamage(crew, amount, cause)`, `DamageTaskSink.post(ship, task)`, and the task classes `SealBreachTask`, `RepairTask`, `ExtinguishTask`, `RescueTask`. `CrewService` implements all three; `CrewFactory.install(engine)` adds the crew systems and returns it.
- **Pathfinding:** `GridNavGraph` computes connections straight from the grid on each search, so doors and destroyed walls need no rebuild step. `CrewMovementSystem` stops a crew member whose next step became blocked and the tree re-plans. Task work spots are found by breadth-first search (`GridPathfinder.nearest`).
- **Behavior tree** (`CrewBehaviorTrees.standard`): a `DynamicGuardSelector` of engage (guarded by line of sight to a hostile), work a board task (claim, walk to the work spot, work, complete; rescues pick up, carry to the nearest safe tile and put down) and idle (leave unsafe tiles). Marines also hunt hostiles any crewmate can see.
- **Task board:** `ShipTaskBoardComponent` on the ship. Crew pick the highest `priority × role affinity − distance`. Posting is idempotent. `ShipHazardScanSystem` posts fire, breach and repair tasks from the grid and cancels stale ones. Boarders (another faction) never take the ship's tasks.
- **Health:** `CrewHealthSystem` runs on the hazard tick: asphyxiation, burns from hot or burning tiles, recovery on safe tiles (faster with a medic's `TreatTask`), and rescue tasks for unconscious crew on unsafe tiles.
- **Interior physics:** inside every ship or station someone is walking there is a Box2D world (`InteriorWorld`, in tile units, no gravity), owned by `CrewService.physics` (`CrewPhysics`). Its walls are static edges along every tile edge crew can't cross: between an open tile and a closed one or the hull's outside, and wherever a wall or shut door stands between two open tiles; a docked port that can be crossed leaves its edge open. The walls are rebuilt on the tick that set changes. Each crew member on their feet on an open tile is a `CrewBody`: a 0.3-tile circle that walls stop, with a solid 0.15-tile core that other crew's cores can't get into. Within 0.4 tiles of each other's middles crew push apart softly, harder the closer they get and a little to each one's right, so two meeting head on in a corridor sidestep. Crew walk by force, up to 25 tiles/s², toward their next tile (AI) or where the input points (avatar); someone standing still drifts back to their spot when pushed off it. A walker who makes no headway toward the next tile for 3 s gives the route up and the tree re-plans. Downed and carried crew have no body, so others step over them; so has the avatar while it sits at a console (`PlayerAvatarComponent.seated`, set by the game's bridge each tick), so crew walking past go by it rather than shove it off its seat (round 7: a yard technician pushed the captain off the helm, and the career's saves loaded with them standing on deck). Out on a docked walkway, or on a tile that isn't open, crew move along without a body as before and take one up again back on the deck. Worlds are made when the first crew member steps on a deck and dropped when the last leaves; a load drops them all.
- **Player avatar:** `PlayerAvatarComponent` marks a crew member moved by input instead of AI; `CrewQueries.playerShipLocal` gives its ship-local position for the HUD and audio listener.
- **NPC pilot** (`pilot` package): `NpcPilotComponent` on a ship with physics-exterior's flight components makes `NpcPilotSystem` fly it through `FlightControlComponent`. It flies its `PilotBurn`s (time, prograde, radial; the cancelled rails burns macro-sim hands over on bubble entry) in order, then either seeks an in-bubble point or departs outward at `departSpeed` until macro-sim puts it back on rails. `PilotReference` supplies sim time and the parent-relative frame; the game wires it to macro-sim's true state, and `PilotReference.bubbleOnly` stands in for tests. The decision logic, `PilotController`, has no Box2D in it.
- **Saves:** `CrewSaveParticipant.install(crew, gameEngine)` makes crew save with core-engine. Saves keep each crew member's stats, health, state, tile and ship (`GridPositionComponent.shipRef`) and NPC pilots' burns. The job in hand is not saved: after a load claimed tasks, carries and treatments are dropped, the task board starts empty, and crew get fresh behavior trees and pick their jobs again from what the hazard scan and health systems post.
- **Timing:** the crew systems and the NPC pilot run on core-engine's fixed sim ticks (`FixedIteratingSystem`), so physics warp speeds up walking, decisions, burns and suffocation along with the hazards.
- **Ship stations:** each crew member has `gunnerySkill` and `trackingSkill` in `CrewDef` (marines are better gunners). `CrewQueries.bestSkill(engine, ship, station)` gives the best conscious crew member's skill (at the weapons console, where there is one; see Manning consoles), and the game's `CrewStationSync` copies it onto the ship's weapon mounts and sensors. A knocked-out crew shoots and tracks like a green one; ships without simulated crew keep their own values.
- **NPC combat pilot** (`pilot` package): `NpcCombatComponent` makes `NpcCombatSystem` fight its `target`. `CombatPilotController` closes to `preferredRange`, holds it while circling with the nose on the target, and breaks across the line of an inbound missile, which the game reports through a `ThreatSensor`. It takes over from `NpcPilotSystem` while on. The game gives it to the raider when the player shoots at it. Its `stance` changes how it holds range: `FLANK` swings round behind the target and holds there, and `KITE` (round 5) holds the range band nose on, with no circling, and backs off `KITE_GAIN` (4) times as hard when the target closes inside it; the game's `Tactics` picks the stance (see progression/Design.md).
- **Across a dock and carrying:** crew plan paths onto another craft through a docked port (`CrewAgent.travel`: walk to the nearest open `GridLink` onto it, then over the collars), and `CrewMemberComponent.home` is the craft they work for. Away from home they take only carry jobs and otherwise walk back. A `CarryTask` (kind `CARRY`) is one load: walk beside a cargo bay of the craft it comes from, take up to `CrewDef.carryVolume` (2 m³) through its `CarryTask.Job`, walk it to a bay of the other craft at `loadSpeedFactor` pace and set it down; a trip in hand when the task is dropped goes back to the job (`CrewService.dropLoad`). The `LOADER` role carries best and fights fires and does rescues at a lower affinity; a crewman will carry, less keenly. `CrewService.hostility` decides which factions fight (any two different ones by default; the game leaves station crew alone).
- **Doors** (`CrewDoors`, on `CrewService.doors`): AI crew path through shut bulkhead doors between two rooms, at 2 tiles' extra cost (`GridNavGraph.DOOR_COST`), stop at the door for 1 s to open it, and shut it again once no one is in the doorway or stepping through next (after at least 0.5 s). Only a door crew opened is shut again; one opened by hand stays open. Crew don't open a hostile craft's doors, unless `CrewService.doorRights` lets them through (core's `Boarding` lets a boarding party through the craft it boards), and don't open a door from breathable air onto air too thin to breathe without a suit on; out of thin air into breathable air is fine. A search takes the crew member's `DoorKey` (`CrewAgent.findPath`, `CrewAgent.nearest`); `GridNavGraph.canStep` still means open right now. Which doors are held open is not saved.
- **Space suits:** every crew member carries one (`CrewDef.suitAir`, 600 s of air; 0 for none). Before going to a task whose spot is out of breathable air (`TaskRules.needsSuit`: a breach, a room losing its air), crew spend `suitTime` (5 s) putting it on (`SuitUp`, ahead of `MoveToWorkSpot` in the rescue and work branches). While suited they walk at `suitSpeedFactor` (0.85) and don't suffocate; the suit's air drains while the air around isn't fit to breathe, and once it is empty they suffocate as usual. Idle on a safe tile for 5 s, they take it off (half the time) and its air is topped up. **At battle stations** (`ShipTaskBoardComponent.battleStations`, set each frame by the game while the ship fights or has been shot at in the last minute) crew suit up for any task and while idle, and keep the suit on, so a breach in their room doesn't suffocate them; suited crew also work at `suitWorkFactor` (0.8). The player turns this off for their ship from the crew window (`suitsAtBattleStations`, saved with the board; on by default). Suited crew may open a door onto thin air when the room beyond is not open to space (the two rooms share their air out); never onto one that is. Suits and their air are saved with the crew member. **Filling a suit (round 6):** below `suitRefillShare` (30%) of its air, out of breathable air, a crew member drops what they are doing (the `[SuitAirLow] RefillSuit` branch, after fighting and before work) and fills it in `suitRefillTime` (20 s) beside an oxygen tank holding some, or a working life support with oxygen piped to it (any working life support on a layout with no oxygen tanks), drawing `SUIT_OXYGEN_KG` (0.05 kg). With no supply aboard reachable they go back to work and look again 10 s later. A ship holed past its patch kits used to leave its crew to suffocate once their suits ran dry, with its life support and tanks working.
- **Boarding and salvage:** a `BoardTask` (kind `BOARD`, priority 30, an order) sends a crew member onto another craft, suited, to a spot in one of its rooms; marines want it most (2), crewmen a little (0.5). Hostiles in sight are fought on the way, as always. Core's `Boarding` posts one per member of the boarding party, each the member's own (`BoardTask.member`; `CrewAgent.mayTake` turns away an order that is someone else's), and the next room as each arrives, so the whole party goes at once however few rooms the craft has, and no one outside it goes (round 6; before, the orders were anyone's and one to a room, so on a raider of one room the crewman at the helm could take the only order and board alone); and holds a taken craft's crew prisoner (`CrewMemberComponent.held`): a held crew member is no one's enemy and takes no work. Engineers also do yard work (1.2), which is how they strip parts off a taken craft (core's `Salvage`), and engineers and marines carry (0.5) as crewmen do, so a crew without crewmen can still fetch salvage and cargo. A `CarryTask` with `fromTile` picks its load up beside a tile rather than at a bay (a stripped part, waiting by its mount).
- **Manning consoles:** `ShipHazardScanSystem` posts a `ManConsoleTask` for each helm and weapons console still standing; crew take one only when no other task suits them (`ShipTaskBoardComponent.best` looks at consoles last). Marines take the weapons console (affinity 1.5) and never the helm; crewmen the helm first (1.2), then the weapons console (1). Crew walk beside it and stay there until any other task on the board would take them, when they step off and the console goes back up for someone else. `CrewQueries.isManning(crew, type)` says who is at which. On a ship with a weapons console, `bestSkill` counts only whoever mans it (and the player's avatar, who is at the controls), so an unmanned console shoots and tracks like a green crew; a ship without one counts anyone aboard, as before. Manning the helm flies the player's ship while the player is away from it (core's `Bridge`): a crewman at a working helm keeps rotation assist on and turns the ship back to the heading the player left it on, without firing the main engine; with nobody at the helm the ship drifts and spins as it was. The starting corvette's crew has a crewman (Bea) for it. While the ship flies and the player isn't at its helm (`ShipTaskBoardComponent.helmWanted`, set by the bridge each tick), a crewman takes the open helm ahead of sitting with a patient (`wantedHelm`): one treating someone steps off to it, and one at it doesn't leave it for a patient. Fires, breaches, bleeding and the rest still come first (round 7: two crew knocked out in a fight kept Bea treating them for days, and the player standing up from the helm found nobody to hand it to).
- **Not yet:** other equipment (suit lockers: for now each crew member carries their own suit); a crewman at the helm flying anywhere on its own (it only holds the heading).
